Ranger is a **fantasy game about raising monsters**. Realism is not a target and
is not a defence.

Do not justify a visual choice by how it compares to the real thing — "thicker
than a real tree of that height", "a real coastline shelves at…". The user pushed
back on exactly that framing for tree trunks: the yardstick is how it **reads on
screen** in a stylised world, next to its touchstones (Pokémon, Monster Rancher),
not what nature does.

**Why:** measuring against reality drags proportions toward the middle and makes
me hedge about exceeding it, when exceeding it is usually the right answer for a
readable stylised look. It also wastes the user's time correcting the frame.

**How to apply:** when tuning a look, say what it should read AS and what it sits
beside in the game. Keep physical models where they buy behaviour that feels right
(the angle of repose makes erosion settle convincingly, offsets-not-heights makes
edits survive re-generation) — the ban is on realism as the *aesthetic* standard,
not on using physics to get good motion.
