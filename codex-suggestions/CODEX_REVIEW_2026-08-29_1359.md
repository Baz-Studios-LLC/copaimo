# Codex review — yard occupation layer and canyon evidence

Scope: read-only review of Claude's current working tree after `8058e6c`. This is advice, not a request to interrupt the active pass. The semantic-yard direction is strong: each yard depicts a purpose, the assets share the town palette and outline treatment, and welding each programme into one object is the right instinct.

## Highest-risk item: bound yards by an authored budget

The current loop turns about 72% of **every candidate lot discarded by building thinning** into a separately spawned yard scene. That makes the final scene count depend on how many provisional lots the street generator happened to produce, not on `HOUSES_IN_A_VILLAGE` / `HOUSES_IN_A_CITY` or an explicit frontage target. If upstream geometry yields hundreds of candidates, a 16- or 34-building settlement can acquire a very large yard population. One welded mesh per yard helps draw cost, but it does not remove scene/entity/transform/visibility/streaming cost, and visual density can overshoot as well.

Suggested implementation shape:

1. Collect eligible unused lots by district.
2. Give each district an explicit occupation target or yard-to-building ratio. Market should normally have the highest frontage occupation, outskirts the lowest; one global `LOTS_LEFT_OPEN` cannot express that hierarchy.
3. Cap the total yard count relative to retained non-yard buildings and/or a measured frontage-length target.
4. Select within that budget deterministically, spread around the ring instead of taking a clump.
5. Record evidence per settlement: candidate lots, retained buildings, yards, open lots, spawned scenes, and approximate triangles. The important invariant is that adding more provisional lot candidates does not silently multiply the shipped scene.

A test worth adding: construct the same settlement plan with an artificially larger candidate-lot set (or expose the selection helper) and assert that final yard count remains within its explicit budget.

## Model and test semantics

- `every_building_has_a_model_on_disk` manually lists the old enum variants and currently omits all five yard variants. Either add them now or centralize an `ALL` list so a future enum addition cannot evade the artefact test.
- `a_town_actually_has_a_town_in_it` uses `layout.plots.len()` as its number of buildings. Yards now make that guard easier to pass even if real buildings regress toward zero. Count `!is_yard()` (and decide explicitly whether landmarks count) for the original invariant; add a separate yard/frontage invariant.
- The district-share denominator was already corrected to exclude yards; good catch. The `here > 3` district-existence assertion still counts yards, so decide whether it is meant to prove district content or actual buildings and name/count accordingly.

## City/village art-family split

The five current programmes are coherent for the village age: timber rail fences, thatch, fruit tree, animal pen, open timber stall, and lean-to work yard. The same models are currently selected for a modern city in Market and Crafts, where they may make the city look accidentally medieval rather than intentionally layered.

Recommended split:

- Village: keep the current garden, pen, timber stall, work yard, and store yard.
- City market: kiosk/awning, planter court, pocket plaza, café or vendor terrace.
- City crafts/service: loading bay, screened utility court, compact storage/service yard.
- City outskirts: community garden or small planted court can reuse the garden idea, but with the city's material family and edge treatment.

If city-specific assets are outside this pass, the safest first scope is to enable rustic yards only for villages and leave city yards open until their programmes match the city's age.

## Collision and deterministic variation

- Returning no walls for every yard makes fences, counters, lean-tos, crates, troughs, and shelters fully ghosted. That will be noticeable at player height. Either give only the large silhouette-defining pieces simple blockers with deliberate entrances, or keep visual barriers low/open enough that crossing them reads as stepping through decoration rather than walking through a solid fence. A player-height walk through all five programmes is the correct acceptance check.
- `yard_for(..., nth)` couples every later yard type to enumeration order: inserting/removing one earlier eligible lot flips the programme of all later lots. Choose the programme from a stable hash of settlement seed + lot identity + district (with a named salt/stream) so local changes stay local.

## Canyon matrix: what the new player-height evidence reveals

The deterministic shots are a real improvement and should stay. They reveal design issues rather than an evidence failure:

- `canyon_east_mouth.png` is nearly a black field under the massif even at fixed noon. The mouth and intended route are not legible.
- `canyon_inside.png` reads as a very broad, empty gray arena with huge dark walls, not yet as a guided winding passage. There is little scale cadence, floor/wall material separation, or mid-distance target.
- `canyon_west_mouth.png` shows the massif's mass, but foreground trees and the oblique wall dominate; the opening itself is not the first read.

Suggested next visual pass after the active town work:

1. Give the traversable floor a distinct, slightly lighter/warm value family from the walls, with a restrained path ribbon or erosion flow that leads the eye.
2. Add sparse authored scale beats: talus groups, strata breaks, ledges, dead vegetation, or rock gates. Concentrate them at bends and decision points rather than uniform scatter.
3. Guarantee a readable light/value portal at each exit from the interior. Terrain self-shadow can remain dramatic, but the route should never collapse into one black mass.
4. For the cel-shaded ink, prioritize the canyon rim silhouette and selective depth/normal discontinuities. Avoid uniform black lines on every terrain triangle; distance-fade secondary creases and keep the floor route cleaner than the walls.
5. Add paired evidence for each mouth: looking in and looking out. The current three shots prove scale, but paired directions will prove entrance recognition and exit pull.
