# Codex review — settlement lighting in progress

Read-only review of `9819ebf` plus the current lamp refinement. The foundation is good: age-specific fittings, authored head/arm contracts, deterministic `--hour` evidence, gradual dusk activation, and a hard budget on real lights. Moving the city light to the end of its arm fixes a real source/fixture mismatch. The latest `night_city.png` has readable pools instead of a uniformly lifted night.

## 1. Hide spatial light-set changes, not only the dusk transition

The dusk fade is smooth, but the nearest-20 selection is spatially discontinuous. When rank 21 becomes rank 20, one child light despawns and another appears at full night intensity. Walking through a dense junction can therefore make a lamp ahead switch on or a nearby pool jump even though time did not change.

Recommended options, from simplest to strongest:

- Fade intensity toward zero over the outer part of `LIT_WITHIN`, so lamps entering/leaving the set are already visually weak.
- Add inner/outer hysteresis: keep an already-lit lamp until it crosses the outer radius; admit replacements inside a smaller radius.
- If the rank cap still switches inside the visible fade zone, retain a small overlap pool and fade ranks near the cap rather than treating 20/21 as binary.

Acceptance evidence must be a slow player-height walk through the city node at night. A still cannot reveal this failure mode.

## 2. Match the emitted light to the fitting

The village lantern is physically an omnidirectional source, so a `PointLight` is a good read. The city fitting is a head on an arm aimed over the carriageway; an omnidirectional point light spends most of its energy upward, backward, and through nearby buildings. With shadows disabled, it can also illuminate surfaces through solid architecture.

Consider a downward/outward `SpotLight` for the city fixture and retain a point light for the village post. This reinforces the two ages at night, produces a carriageway-shaped pool, and reduces implausible façade/back-side leakage. If one light type is kept for simplicity, explicitly inspect from behind a building and beside a wall; the current overhead view cannot prove leakage is acceptable.

Shadows-off is a reasonable performance default. If contact still feels weightless, try shadows only on the one or two closest lights rather than enabling them for all twenty.

## 3. Separate the visible bulb from the expensive illumination

Every physical fitting can show a cheap emissive source cue even when only twenty create real scene lighting. A small shared emissive mesh/sprite at the same authored `(arm, head)` position would:

- keep distant lamps reading as lamps instead of dark poles;
- preserve the street's night rhythm beyond the real-light radius;
- make nearest-set changes much harder to notice;
- give the semi-cel silhouette a controlled bright focal point.

Keep it compact and restrained; it should read as a luminous head, not a large billboard. Bloom, if present, should be subtle and secondary to the drawn silhouette.

## 4. Brightness rationale and night color language

The current Bevy 0.16.1 source documents `PointLight::intensity` as lumens and calls its 1,000,000-lumen default a very large cinema light chosen for the default overcast-day exposure. Therefore “120,000 is a candle” is not technically true. The new value may still be right for this game's exposure and stylization, but tune it from repeatable evidence and describe it as an exposure-compensated art value rather than physical lamp output.

The two ages currently share `LAMPLIGHT`. Consider making the village distinctly amber and the city a slightly cooler warm-white. The fitting silhouettes already establish the split by day; color can preserve it at night without making the city clinically blue.

## 5. Evidence to add before calling the pass complete

Use fixed-hour, player-height shots for both ages:

1. Dusk entrance, proving the fade complements remaining sky light.
2. Full-night node, proving navigation and landmark hierarchy.
3. Behind/alongside a building, checking no-shadow leakage.
4. A slow walk through a junction, checking culling pop and pool continuity.

The aerial images are useful coverage checks, but the player experiences lamp height, glare, occlusion, and switching from the ground.

Optional later polish: a sparse, stable subset of illuminated windows would make the city feel inhabited; avoid lighting every window into a uniform grid.
